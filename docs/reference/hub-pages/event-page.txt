{#day}  How the day works

## {#dayTitle} Turn up with nothing. Leave having raced a robot.

Four stages, one Saturday. You don't need to prepare anything — but the Resource Hub is there if you want a head start.

01

### Briefing

30 minutes. Every diagram prepared for you — motors, driver, IMU, sensors, power, and the maze-solving strategies worth trying.

02

### Build sprint

Six hours to design, solder and program your mouse. Mentors on the floor the whole time — stuck for a minute, not an hour.

03

### Practice mazes

Full-size and mini mazes open all day. Tune against real walls instead of discovering the bug during your timed run.

04

### The race

Classic 16 × 16 maze. Fastest verified run to the centre takes it, with further awards for design and reliability.

{#challenge}

 The challenge

## {#challengeTitle} You're supposed to figure it out

Micromouse is one of the oldest challenges in robotics: build a small robot that solves a maze entirely on its own. No remote control, no driver, no human input once the run starts. The mouse maps the maze, works out the fastest route to the centre, and drives it.

Most Micromouse competitions are won by machines that took months to build. Ours is not one of those.

Every team gets six hours and the same box of parts. The build sprint is the competition — so a first year who has never touched a soldering iron lines up against a final-year team on genuinely equal terms.

- Encoded motors

- Distance sensors

- Battery management

## {#prepTitle} You are never handed a bag of parts and left to it

Engineering and computer science undergraduates, and anyone else who wants a go. No robotics background, no embedded C, nothing to bring. Complete beginners are exactly who we built this for.

Before

A pre-recorded video series on driving the sensors with the ESP32, plus setup docs published in advance — so you don't lose an hour to toolchains.

On the day

Full briefing with diagrams, GitHub basics for anyone who hasn't used it, and a walk through maze-solving strategies so you pick an approach rather than guess.

Throughout

Committee members and postgraduate demonstrators on the floor all day. Stuck? Someone is standing next to you within a minute.

{#judging}

 Judging

### {#judgingTitle} Fastest verified run wins

The main award goes to the fastest verified run to the centre, with further awards for design and reliability.

We run the classic 16 × 16 standard. Attempts, timing and what happens if you touch the mouse are all set out in the competition guide <micromouse-guide.html>.

 The diagonal maze

### {#diagonalTitle} If it can reason about the path, it wins

One mini maze is built so the fastest route cuts the diagonals. A mouse that only follows the grid will finish. A mouse that thinks will beat it — and that maze is open all day so you can find out which one you built.

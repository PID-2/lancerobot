# 2S Battery to 5V Buck Converter

 Steps the 2S battery (7.4 V nominal, 8.4 V full) down to a stable 5.0 V rail for the controller and logic.

 01 Power path

[IMG src=../assets/micromouse/diagrams/2S-5V-buck-power-flow.svg alt='Power path from a protected 2S battery through a buck converter to the ESP32-C6 and 3.3 V sensors']

Protected battery, fuse and switch, then the buck converter, then the ESP32-C6 and 3.3 V sensors.

| IN+ / IN- | Protected battery output, through a fuse and switch |

| OUT+ | 5 V rail and the ESP32-C6 5V pin |

| OUT- | Common GND |

A buck converter is not a charger or battery protection. Use a protected 2S pack or BMS, the right 2S charger, a fuse and a main switch. Motor VM comes from the motor power path, not this logic rail.

 02 Set 5.0 V before connecting anything

- Disconnect the ESP32, sensors and driver from the output.

- Check battery polarity and power only the input.

- Measure across OUT+ and OUT- and turn the trimmer slowly until the meter reads 5.00 V.

- Switch off, connect the load, switch on and measure again.

Never connect USB and the external 5 V rail to the ESP32-C6 at the same time.

 03 Check it under load

- Rail stays at 5 V and the 3.3 V rail does not sag when the motors run.

- Motor noise does not reset the ESP32 or corrupt sensor readings.

- Module and wiring stay cool over a full-length slot.

- Choosing a module: MP1584EN is compact, LM2596 is larger with easier terminals. Size it for controller peak + sensors + margin, and test it under the real load.

If the controller resets when the motors start, measure the rail and the battery during acceleration and fix the wiring, grounding or decoupling. Do not raise the output above 5 V.
